# Episode II mission script — THE HERALD'S ROAD
### Composed by Fable, 2026-07-30, from the full read of *Shadow of
### Ikral* (RC). Canon-interior like The Cubs of Thas: this is Yehu's
### **unnamed dangerous mission** (Ikral ch. 4–6 — Matisyahu fasts for
### him daily at the temple; a messenger reports his return as Yosef
### reaches Nurah; the mission itself is never shown). El Roi's charter
### for it is spoken in-canon (ch. 2): *"You shall travel to unsafe
### lands and you shall stand before rulers who do not know me. But I
### will be with you and watch over you. You shall return, though the
### journey will prove perilous."* Nothing below contradicts a sentence
### of either episode.

## The mission's identity (why this is the one)

King Anayah's problem after Thas: the barels judged a Pyrran capital
while **human soldiers** stood inside its walls. If the Pyrran Clans
read Thas as a human-tuon act of war, Torial faces the cats united.
But Episode I left the door: **Keanna is herald "of El Roi and of the
Pyrran Elders and the Prince"** — and Namal publicly defied that
Prince ("he was not born a king… he does not rule over us") the day
before his city fell. The Prince's word is suddenly the most
vindicated word in Pyrr.

So Anayah sends the one Torialite the Prince's own Judge owes her
life to. **Yehu rides to the Prince of Pyrr** — unsafe lands, a ruler
who does not know Yah Elyon — to give a true account of Thas before
the Clans write their own. The charter fits like a scabbard: perilous
road, pagan court, promised return.

## Cast (all canonical)
- **Yehu** — commander of a thousand; bearer of Keanna's blessing.
- **Keanna** — Judge of the Pyrran Clans; last seen returning to Thas
  to tend survivors; the natural escort and door-opener at court.
- **The Prince of Pyrr** — Namal's speech (Ep I ch. 6) gives his whole
  political position: an Elder among Elders, not a born king, ruling
  "a small clan from an insignificant land," pressing the clans to
  relent and return what is stolen. Never named in canon — the script
  keeps him "the Prince" (composing no name; Sean may grant one).
- **Og** (Ikral ch. 1, 3, 6) — the road passes Lyssa's countryside;
  the Ikral network gives the road its teeth. (Yehu and Og never meet
  in canon — the script keeps it that way; the ambush is Og's *men*,
  Pilisian zealots, not Og himself.)
- **Matisyahu** — stays in Nurah (canon: he is at Rhea/along the
  King's Highway in this window); his *fasting* is the mission's
  unseen second thread, and the game shows its weight at the end.

## Flags
`Escort` (Keanna rides along), `RoadBlood` (fought the zealots),
`TruthTold` / `TruthShaded` (the account given at court), `Bowed`
(the Ikral-courtesy trap), `PrinceHeart` (the private audience).

## Scenes

**0 — The King's Commission** *(Nurah, the throne room, night)*
Anayah, quietly, off the record: "The cats will decide this season
what Thas *meant*. I would have them hear it from the man who was
inside it. You will carry no treaty, Commander — only the truth, told
whole. That is the more dangerous cargo."
→ [Take the road south] 

**1 — The Judge at the Ford** *(the border river)*
Keanna waits at the crossing as if appointed — "El Roi mentioned you
would be passing." CHOICE: ask her escort (sets `Escort` — the court
scenes soften; the road scenes sharpen, for her presence draws the
zealots' eyes) or decline to spare her (she smiles: "Then I will
merely travel the same road at the same hour").

**2 — The Burned Waystation** *(the King's Highway, Lyssan country)*
The parasa post stands abandoned, scorched — Ikral's mark chalked on
the door (ties to Matisyahu's ch. 6 report: "the last post we passed
was abandoned"). Reading the signs. CHOICE: press on by night /
camp and watch (watching reveals the zealots' numbers — information
that changes scene 3's options).

**3 — The Zealots of the Grove** *(the dark forest before the border)*
Og's Pilisians — red-haired men with bull-amulets — bar the road:
"The cat-woman is a blasphemer of Ikral, and you her dog." CHOICE:
fight (combat set-piece → `RoadBlood`; Keanna wounded if `Escort`) /
the Matisyahu gambit — invoke the king's wrath and Ikral's own honor
("if Ikral is offended, let him strike us himself") — works only if
scene 2 was *watched* (you know their leader fears the king's name).

**4 — The Court of the Prince** *(Pyrr, the small hall of a small clan)*
No throne — a mat, a low table, the Prince pouring tea himself; the
Elders watching from the walls. He asks one question: "Tell me what
my Judge could not see from inside a cell: *why did your people come
to Thas?*" CHOICE — the mission's spine:
- **The whole truth** (`TruthTold`): the cubs, the ruse, the barels —
  and that the judgment was no human's doing and no human's to claim.
- **The polished truth** (`TruthShaded`): the cubs and the rescue,
  the judgment left vague — safer, and a lie by shape.

**5 — The Courtesy Trap** *(the feast that follows)*
An Elder of another clan — an Ikral-sympathizer among the maeuw —
proposes the guests honor "the gods of every land" with a poured
libation. The court watches. CHOICE: pour (sets `Bowed` — the Elders
warm, Keanna goes still) / set the cup down untouched: "I am the
king's man, and my God watches me even here" (the Naaman-scene
inverted; the Prince's ears sharpen).

**6 — The Private Audience** *(the Prince's garden, before dawn)*
Granted only if `TruthTold` and not `Bowed`. The Prince, alone: "My
word was despised in Thas until the day it was vindicated in fire. I
do not know your god. But I know that what I commanded and what he
judged were the same thing. Tell me, soldier — does El Roi *see* a
maeuw?" The player answers with Keanna's own commission from Ep I
ch. 6 — "he told her to find the Witness of Thas, *for his clan needs
him*" — the proof the question was already answered. Sets
`PrinceHeart`.

**7 — The Road Home / Endings**
- **THE OPEN DOOR** (`TruthTold`, no `Bowed`, `PrinceHeart`): the
  Prince sends Yehu home with a small carved token — "for the day my
  clans and your king must speak again" — and Keanna's last word:
  "Matisyahu's fasting was not wasted, though he will never know what
  it bought." *(The player learns what the novella's temple-scenes
  meant.)*
- **THE SEALED ACCOUNT** (`TruthShaded`): peace, of a thinner kind;
  the Prince bows exactly as low as protocol requires; Keanna rides
  home silent. "You told them a smaller Thas than the one that
  happened. Smaller truths buy smaller peaces."
- **THE POURED CUP** (`Bowed`): the Elders toast the flexible human;
  the Prince does not appear again; on the road home the burned
  waystation is manned once more — by men wearing bull-amulets openly.
  A cold coda: some doors close politely.

## Mechanics notes (for the wiring session)
- Same machinery as The Cubs of Thas: pages + choices + flags on
  `model.states`; entry from a new "Episode II" item after the feast
  (or a chapter-select on the title once two episodes exist).
- Scene 3's combat can reuse the battle FSM with the `OnRoad` return
  pattern; zealot stats = Ruffian stats re-skinned until a Pilisian
  entity is designed (queued).
- The scene-2 → scene-3 information dependency is one flag
  (`Watched`) gating a choice's *availability* — the first time a
  choice is hidden rather than present-but-wrong; needs a third
  choice slot on the mission view (the dispute's mC already exists).
- Endings deliberately rhyme with Episode I's: wait-for-the-sign →
  tell-the-whole-truth; the moral spine graduates from *patience* to
  *candor*.

## Composer's honesty block
The Prince, his garden, the courtesy trap, and all dialogue are
composed; every constraint they sit inside is canonical (listed
above). The Naaman-inversion and the Matisyahu-fasting payoff are
Fable's readings of Sean's own devices (the meal-diplomacy of Ikral
ch. 2; the temple-fasting of ch. 5) — red pen owns every word. The
mission deliberately does NOT show Lyssa, Og in person, Yosef, or
Nathan: their arcs belong to the novella, and the game walks between
them, not over them.
